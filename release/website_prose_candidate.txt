Abstract
Abstract
Can a general-purpose reasoning model do more than generate plausible 3D assets and instead act as an engineering reasoner that tests and revises modeling hypotheses when physical evidence contradicts them? We study this question in a longitudinal reconstruction of one operating 220 kV substation. GPT-6 Astra, accessed through Codex, operates inside a fixed Blender/Python harness that supplies registered photogrammetric geometry, field images, engineering records, prior reconstruction state, deterministic tools, and task-specific validators. The preserved record contains 38 sequential core batches across 12 task families; later batches inherit earlier geometry, reusable assets, validators, and failures, so the study is not an IID benchmark.
The record reveals a useful separation. When a task can be formulated as an explicit measurement problem, Astra can select a local reference and delegate numerical fitting to deterministic operators. The harder revisions occur at the hypothesis level. Among the seven documented adaptation/failure episodes selected for cross-case analysis, five concern the evaluation protocol, reusable-asset boundary, representation class, or task-selection rule. B15 revises an overgeneralized shared asset; B23 replaces a straight lead after measured profiles expose approximately 0.5 m of curvature; B25/B26 changes task selection from inventory completion to unexplained geometry; and D38 converts 2D review markup into 3D correction but then requires a second review to repair an orientation-semantics error. These cases show both the value and the limit of model reasoning: externalized evidence can trigger higher-level engineering revisions, while first-pass fine detail and coordinate semantics remain error-prone.
Accepted task outputs accumulate into a 37,153-object station state, which we use as a long-horizon stress test of reuse, provenance, and composition rather than as an accuracy benchmark. The evidence therefore supports a narrow characterization: Astra can participate in a persistent, evidence-grounded 3D engineering process and revise high-level modeling decisions when contradictions are made explicit, but deterministic validation and human review remain material to acceptance. The present record is limited to one site, has no independent survey reference, and contains no matched reruns with another model.
1 · Study Question and Evaluation Setting
A persistent engineering environment for observing model decisions
The focal model is GPT-6 Astra. The Blender/Python harness defines what evidence the model can inspect, what programmatic actions it can take, and how outputs are checked and retained.
Provenance and inference boundary. The historical reconstruction used Codex + GPT-6 Astra with Extra High reasoning effort as owner-confirmed experimental metadata; legacy batch files do not freeze this configuration. They also do not preserve private chain-of-thought. The observable record consists of selected evidence, authored or revised programs, deterministic outputs, validation results, review artifacts, and subsequent revisions.
For batch t, Astra receives a bounded context assembled from registered photogrammetric geometry, field images, engineering or semantic records, spatial priors, and the persistent state left by earlier batches. Its observable action is programmatic: it writes or revises Blender/Python code, selects numerical operators, requests renders or diagnostics, and interprets the resulting evidence. Numerical fitting, rendering, collision/contact checks, hash preservation, and task-native validation execute outside the language model.
This separation is essential to the research question. A small residual produced by SciPy is not evidence that Astra performed numerical optimization internally; it is evidence that Astra formulated and invoked a particular measurement procedure. Conversely, a plausible Blender program is not accepted because it was authored by the model. It enters persistent state only after the corresponding review and validator checks.

2 · Evaluation Tasks and Protocol
Twelve reconstruction task families as behavioral probes
The task suite spans 38 dependent core batches in one operating substation. It probes how the same model/harness handles different engineering decisions; it is not an IID benchmark and has no global success-rate denominator.
The sequence also records a change in reconstruction scope. B01–B19 concentrate on individual devices and reusable equipment families. B20–B30 add explicit bus and conductor connections and use coverage analysis to identify unresolved geometry. B31–D38 close civil and auxiliary structures and incorporate reviewer-identified omissions. D40 preserves geometry while changing presentation materials, and D41 adds transformer inspection semantics.

5 · Cross-Case Findings
What Astra does well, and where the workflow still fails
These findings are synthesized after the cases. They characterize the preserved record and selected diagnostic episodes; they do not estimate population-wide failure frequencies.
Figure 6. Descriptive context for the cross-case analysis. Visible revision tags are a lower bound; failure-layer and adaptation labels are manual annotations of seven documented episodes; operator counts describe recorded workflow stages rather than success rates.
5.1 Explicit measurement operators separate engineering reasoning from numerical estimation
Wall, capacitor, building, and B32 cabin tasks obtain centimeter-scale internal agreement when the workflow first compiles a registered local comparison domain and then delegates fitting to deterministic code. The numerical fit is therefore not an Astra capability by itself. The model contribution visible in the record is choosing what geometric quantity to estimate, what evidence belongs to the domain, how the domain is split, and which operator is invoked.
5.2 The difficult revisions occur above parameter tuning
Among the seven annotated adaptation/failure episodes, five are assigned to protocol, abstraction, representation, or task-selection layers. B01 changes the comparison domain; B15 changes the MASTER/site boundary; B23 changes the representation class; B25/B26 changes how the next task is chosen; D38.1 corrects a coordinate-semantics interpretation. The common feature is that changing a dimension would not have repaired the underlying assumption. This five-of-seven count applies only to the selected documented episodes.
5.3 First-pass fine detail and coordinate semantics remain error-prone
B32 reaches a defensible cabin envelope while still placing doors incorrectly. B23 inherits a geometrically inadequate straight connection. D38.1 turns a successful image registration into the wrong equipment orientation. In each case the output is plausible enough to survive initial construction but is contradicted by an externalized review artifact. This makes profiles, fixed-camera overlays, and task-specific diagnostics part of the measurement process rather than presentation material.
5.4 Human input is sparse but materially changes the trajectory
B08 requires explicit authorization before the two transformer assemblies are treated as equivalent; B36 begins from an omission report; D38 begins from marked review images. The human does not edit mesh vertices, but these interventions change what may be shared, what subsystem is reconstructed, or which region is corrected. The demonstrated system is therefore agent-driven and human-steerable rather than autonomous.
5.5 Persistent state enables composition and also propagates abstraction errors
Inherited geometry, reusable masters, protected history, and prior validators allow later batches to build on earlier work. The same persistence makes a wrong abstraction consequential: B15 propagates finite geometry through a shared master until the reuse boundary is revised. Long-horizon state is therefore both an enabling mechanism and a risk surface. Provenance, quarantine, and geometry-preserving version boundaries are required to keep later work interpretable.
6 · Long-Horizon Composition Stress Test
Can accepted decisions survive composition?
The full station is used here as a stress test of persistent state: reuse boundaries, transforms, connections, provenance, and rejected variants must remain interpretable as later tasks modify the same project.
At the D38.2 geometry baseline, accepted outputs from the preceding task sequence coexist in one editable Blender state. The D38.2/D40-equivalent project contains 37,153 objects, 3,916 mesh datablocks, 755 scenes, and 858 collections. These are scale descriptors, not counts of independently verified physical components. Their role in the argument is to show that earlier assets are not discarded when the experiment moves to a new task.
The more informative checks concern state discipline. Reusable masters must remain shared only where equivalence is justified; finite site-specific geometry must stay local; connection endpoints must still meet equipment created in earlier batches; and failed or superseded variants remain in history. D40 then changes only presentation materials while preserving geometry, transforms, object count, and vertex count. D41 adds semantics after that boundary rather than rewriting the reconstruction record.

6.2 · Composition Endpoint
D41 adds inspection-addressable parts without rewriting geometry history
D41 is an additive endpoint rather than a second reconstruction study. After the main geometry stabilizes, it adds 79 accessory meshes spanning 17 selected inspection-relevant transformer part classes and instantiates the shared accessory collection on both T1 and T2. The validation record reports 79/79 selected meshes and 17/17 selected classes on both instances. This demonstrates one downstream use of the component hierarchy; it does not establish complete station-wide semantics.

7 · Discussion
Engineering reasoning is useful when its assumptions are externalized
The longitudinal record supports a narrower claim than autonomous reconstruction. Astra repeatedly participates in decisions that determine what is measured, which representation is built, what may be reused, and which unresolved region becomes the next task. Several cases then show the same model making plausible but incorrect decisions at those levels. The practical value of the system therefore comes from coupling model reasoning to measurements, review artifacts, deterministic checks, and a persistent history that makes later contradiction possible.
This is also why the comparison with the reference robot-policy reports is methodological rather than statistical. Their task suites probe one policy and their behavioral episodes explain aggregate outcomes; here, reconstruction tasks probe one persistent model/harness and the episodes explain changes in engineering decisions. The difference matters: these batches are dependent, later tasks inherit earlier state, and validators are heterogeneous because the geometry under test is heterogeneous.
7.2 · Threats to Validity
Limitations and unresolved comparisons
The current evidence characterizes one model-in-harness record; it does not rank alternatives or establish cross-site accuracy.
Single-site external validity. All task families come from one substation; diversity within one site does not establish transfer.
No independent survey reference. Headline centimeter-scale residuals compare against the same mesh that guided reconstruction. They are not independent survey accuracy.
Heterogeneous task-native validators. Appropriate for an engineering case study; it prevents reduction to one stationary benchmark success rate.
Human steering is material. Equivalence authorization, omission review, image markup, and semantic audit all influenced the trajectory.
Owner-confirmed model provenance. Historical logs do not freeze model identifier or reasoning effort. Future studies should log model/version, reasoning setting, tool versions, evidence manifest, token usage, wall-clock, human interventions, and output hashes per run.
Behavioral mining is filename-level. Revision counts are lower bounds; failure-mode and adaptation labels are manual annotations of documented episodes, reported with their sources, not derived measurements.
No matched model comparison. The historical record contains no controlled rerun with a second foundation model, specialized 3D system, or professional CAD baseline. Any claim of relative model superiority would therefore exceed the evidence.
